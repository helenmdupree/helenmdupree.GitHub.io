About · Reading & Influence · Books That Stayed

Books that stayed.

Books whose ideas continued to surface in the way Helen M. Dupree thinks about growth, leadership, value, relationships, and scale.

BOOKS THAT STAYED →

Build something great.
Build something capable of scaling.
Never forget that business is ultimately between people.

Some books are useful in the moment. Others keep showing up years later in the way a person thinks, leads, sells, builds, and makes decisions.

BOOK COVERGood to GreatJim Collins
DISCIPLINED GROWTH

Good to Great

Jim Collins

In 2010, when Helen joined MATCOR, then-CEO Jeff Stello gave every employee a copy of Good to Great. It became more than a business book. It helped establish an enduring belief that sustainable growth is not created by chasing the next opportunity, but through disciplined choices about people, focus, execution, and what a company is capable of becoming.

What stayed: Great companies are built deliberately. Growth without discipline is simply activity.

BOOK COVERThe Science of ScalingDr. Benjamin Hardy & Blake Erickson
SCALABLE THINKING

The Science of Scaling

Dr. Benjamin Hardy & Blake Erickson

The Science of Scaling challenged Helen to think beyond incremental growth. Its lasting idea is that meaningful scale does not necessarily come from doing more of what already works. Sometimes it requires redesigning the model, eliminating what cannot scale, and setting a goal large enough to demand a fundamentally different way of thinking.

What stayed: A bigger future often requires a different system, not simply more effort.

BOOK COVERThe Go-GiverBob Burg & John David Mann
VALUE & RELATIONSHIPS

The Go-Giver

Bob Burg & John David Mann

Some business books teach strategy. The Go-Giver is a reminder of what business is ultimately for. Its principles of value, influence, and authenticity reinforce a belief Helen has carried throughout her career: some of the strongest commercial relationships begin when the objective stops being the transaction and becomes helping another person succeed.

What stayed: Value comes before reward. Trust and influence are earned through what one contributes.
