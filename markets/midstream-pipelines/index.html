Midstream & Pipelines

Pipeline operations connect integrity, reliability, regulation, field execution, technology, and commercial decisions across assets that may span thousands of miles.

Explore the operating realityOpen Resources

OPERATING REALITY

One indication can move through many decisions before the work is complete.

A corrosion indication can become an integrity assessment, an excavation, a maintenance decision, a regulatory record, a budget decision, and an input to the next assessment. The strongest programs keep the evidence, engineering judgment, field execution, and resulting record connected.

Signal→Evidence→Integrity Decision→Field Work→Verification→Learning

WHAT IS CHANGING

More information raises the standard for how decisions are made.

EVIDENCE

Integrity programs are becoming more evidence-intensive.

Inspection, material records, corrosion data, operating history, field findings, and engineering analysis increasingly have to support a decision that another qualified reviewer can understand and reconstruct.

ANALYTICS

Better models still depend on credible inputs.

More sophisticated analysis can improve decision quality, but uncertainty in flaw sizing, material properties, operating context, or growth assumptions does not disappear because the mathematics improved.

FIELD EXECUTION

The decision becomes real in the field.

Excavation, direct examination, repair, mitigation, monitoring, and documentation are where planning meets asset reality. Field findings can confirm, challenge, or refine what was believed beforehand.

TECHNOLOGY

Digital capability has to fit the integrity workflow.

Data integration, remote monitoring, analytics, digital twins, and AI are most useful when they reduce decision friction, improve traceability, or create a better window for action.

SOUBEL PERSPECTIVE

Better information alone is not the finish line.

Operational Trust exists when the evidence can be understood, the decision can be defended, the work can be executed, and the result can be verified. That same discipline applies to integrity programs, technology adoption, and the commercial case for products and services entering the midstream market.

Can the evidence, decision, work, and record tell the same story?

That question reaches beyond compliance. It tests whether the organization can move from technical information to action without losing the reasoning that made the action defensible in the first place.
