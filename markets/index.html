OPPORTUNITY DEVELOPMENT

Where Commercial Opportunities Begin

Commercial growth can come from solving what the customer already knows about, uncovering what has not yet been recognized, or helping the customer prepare for what comes next.

01

Recognized Need

The customer already knows there is a problem, project, requirement, or objective. The commercial work is understanding the situation, the people involved, the timing, and what will move the decision forward.

02

Unrecognized or Emerging Need

The need exists, but the customer has not fully identified, connected, or quantified it yet. The commercial work is helping make the risk, cost, inefficiency, lost value, or opportunity visible enough to act on.

03

Future Opportunity

Nothing has to be wrong today. Growth can come from expansion, modernization, standardization, new technology, efficiency goals, regulation, or preparing for what comes next.

FROM OPPORTUNITY TO DECISION

What moves an opportunity forward?

Once an opportunity is identified, the commercial work shifts to understanding what will make it relevant, actionable, and fundable inside the customer’s organization.

WHY

Why act?

Define the business outcome. The reason may be lower risk, reduced cost, improved reliability, increased capacity, regulatory readiness, better visibility, stronger performance, or a new source of value.

→
WHO

Who is involved?

Identify who owns the problem or opportunity, who will use the solution, who influences the technical decision, who controls the budget, and who approves the purchase.

→
WHEN

When can it move?

Understand the timing: budget cycles, outages, turnarounds, capital plans, contract renewals, regulatory deadlines, planned projects, and competing operational priorities.

→
WHERE

Where do I enter?

Enter where the need is real — field, engineering, integrity, maintenance, operations, procurement, IT, or leadership — then connect the opportunity to the people who can move it forward.

→
HOW

How does it become a decision?

Build credibility, connect technical capability to a business outcome, quantify value where possible, establish proof, address objections, and give the buying group enough confidence to act.

A PERSONAL NOTE

From vendor to partner has never been a sales slogan to me. It is how long-term relationships and revenue are built.

I have spent much of my career walking into businesses, markets, and accounts where there was something to build.

Sometimes the opportunity was obvious. Sometimes it had to be uncovered. And sometimes the first job was simply earning enough trust to understand what the customer really needed.

I have never believed that great commercial work begins with a product sheet. It begins with curiosity and listening — understanding the operation, the people, the pressures, the economics, and what the customer is trying to accomplish.

The strongest customer relationships are the ones where someone knows they can call me at 8:00 p.m. on a Saturday — whether there is a purchase order involved or not.

Helen
