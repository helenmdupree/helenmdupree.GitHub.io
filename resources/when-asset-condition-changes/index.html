PRACTICAL LEADERSHIP GUIDE

When Asset Condition Changes

10 Questions for a Defensible Decision. A practical framework for moments when asset condition, performance, or evidence changes and a decision has to be made before certainty is available.

Download the 3-page guideAll SOUBEL resources

WHY IT EXISTS

Better decisions start with better questions.

A changed signal does not automatically mean the asset changed. A technically plausible interpretation is not automatically the right business decision. And a decision that was reasonable yesterday may need to change when new evidence arrives.

This guide provides a concise sequence for testing the signal, challenging the interpretation, understanding consequence, comparing legitimate options, and defining what evidence should trigger reconsideration.

THE FRAMEWORK

Ten questions to carry into the decision.

The web version makes the framework visible and scannable. The downloadable guide provides the printable working format.

What changed?

Define the observation precisely: signal, trend, inspection finding, field observation, test result, model output, or performance shift.

Can we trust the evidence?

Check source, quality, completeness, calibration, timing, lineage, assumptions, and whether the apparent change may reflect the data rather than the asset.

What was happening when it changed?

Establish operating mode, load, pressure, temperature, throughput, environment, recent work, process changes, and other relevant context.

What other evidence agrees — or disagrees?

Integrate inspection, integrity, maintenance, process, field, laboratory or metallurgical, historical, and comparable-asset evidence where applicable.

What does it mean for intended function?

Connect condition to asset duty, criticality, degradation or failure context, reliability, availability, and performance.

What happens if our interpretation is wrong?

Challenge potential safety, environmental, regulatory, production, reliability, financial, reputational, and other material consequences.

What legitimate options exist?

Consider continued operation, monitoring, additional evidence, inspection, operating changes, escalation, mitigation, repair, deferral, replacement, redesign, or specialist analysis.

Why is one option preferable now?

Compare risk, cost, performance, timing, uncertainty, downtime, resource constraints, remaining life, and reversibility.

What would cause us to change the decision?

State assumptions, contradictory evidence, information gaps, thresholds, triggers, and escalation conditions.

How will we know the decision worked?

Define ownership, follow-up evidence, effectiveness measures, verification, record updates, and learning.
