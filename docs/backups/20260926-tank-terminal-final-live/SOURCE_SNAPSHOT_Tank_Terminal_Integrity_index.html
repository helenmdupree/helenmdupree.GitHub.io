01 · TANK INTEGRITY

Tanks have several integrity stories happening at once.

A tank is not one inspection surface. Bottom, shell, roof, foundation, appurtenances, product service, and corrosion-control systems can experience different damage mechanisms, different rates of change, and different inspection constraints.

BOTTOM & FOUNDATION

Condition starts below the visible surface.

Soil-side corrosion, internal corrosion, water bottoms, underside exposure, floor condition, annular areas, settlement, foundation performance, leak evidence, coatings, linings, and cathodic protection all affect the tank-bottom integrity picture.

SHELL & ROOF

Thickness is only one part of condition.

Atmospheric and internal corrosion, weld condition, distortion, wind and vacuum effects, roof drainage, floating-roof components, seals, nozzles, penetrations, stairs, platforms, and attachments all contribute evidence.

SERVICE & HISTORY

What the tank has experienced changes what the evidence means.

Product, water, temperature, fill cycles, operating level, modifications, repairs, previous inspection results, corrosion rates, coating history, and known damage mechanisms belong with the current finding.

ENGINEERING DECISION

Inspection results have to lead somewhere.

Remaining life, suitability for continued service, fitness-for-service evaluation, repair scope, inspection interval, mitigation, return-to-service, and reassessment depend on qualified interpretation of the evidence.

02 · INSPECTION & CONDITION EVIDENCE

Use the method that can answer the integrity question.

Inspection credibility depends on what is being examined, the damage mechanism of concern, access, tank configuration, service conditions, and the limitations of the method. No single technique provides the whole condition picture.

03 · TRANSFER, CONTAINMENT & RESPONSE

The integrity boundary extends into the systems around the tank.

Tank condition is only part of the operating risk. Product moves through piping and equipment, is controlled through instrumentation and procedures, and is bounded by containment and response systems when something goes wrong.

TRANSFER & MECHANICAL SYSTEMS

Buried and aboveground equipment face different threats.

Piping, pumps, valves, manifolds, meters, loading arms, hoses, dead legs, low points, supports, isolation, vibration, leakage, coatings, CP where applicable, and maintenance history affect reliability and containment.

OVERFILL & SECONDARY CONTAINMENT

Protection depends on more than one device.

Operating limits, tank capacity, level measurement, alarms, independent protection, shutdown actions, transfer rates, testing, dikes, berms, drainage, penetrations, and containment condition work together.

RELEASE RESPONSE

The response begins before the release.

Isolation, shutdown capability, communications, spill planning, access, procedures, maintenance condition, contractor readiness, and post-event learning determine how effectively consequence can be controlled.

04 · APPLICATION ENVIRONMENTS

Tank integrity shows up in more than one industry.

The tank may be similar, but the service, consequence, inspection strategy, and operating context may not be. A credible integrity approach starts with the asset and then accounts for where and how it is used.

PETROLEUM TERMINALS & TANK FARMS

Bulk storage, receipt, transfer, loading, overfill prevention, containment, buried and aboveground piping, corrosion control, and emergency response.

REFINING & PETROCHEMICAL

Storage tied to process units, changing feedstocks and products, atmospheric and internal corrosion, inspection planning, turnaround work, and mechanical-integrity requirements.

MIDSTREAM & PIPELINE FACILITIES

Breakout tanks, terminals, compressor and pump stations, gathering and transmission interfaces, receipts and deliveries, corrosion-control systems, and transfer equipment.

UTILITIES & INDUSTRIAL INFRASTRUCTURE

Fuel and chemical storage, treatment systems, backup-power fuel, buried infrastructure, facility piping, containment, monitoring, and reliability requirements.

MANUFACTURING & PROCESS FACILITIES

Raw-material, intermediate, chemical, fuel, and product storage linked to process reliability, materials compatibility, inspection, maintenance, and environmental controls.

WATER & WASTEWATER

Where applicable, steel storage, coatings and linings, corrosion control, structural condition, inspection, and lifecycle maintenance within a different regulatory and service context.

05 · STANDARDS & REGULATION

Use current primary sources to govern the requirement.

Tank integrity is governed within a standards and regulatory framework that varies with service, tank type, jurisdiction, material, and facility configuration. Current source material should govern the requirement, and qualified engineering judgment should govern how it is applied.

U.S. EPATank Inspections

Current SPCC guidance for integrity inspection of qualified aboveground oil storage containers, including use of industry standards.

 STI/SPFASP001

Standard for inspection of aboveground storage tanks, including many shop-fabricated tanks, small field-erected tanks, portable containers, and associated secondary containment.

 STI/SPFAStandards & Regulatory Resources

Steel-tank standards, codes, regulatory resources, and links to current tank-industry technical context.

 STI/SPFASteel Facts & Technical Resources

Technical material covering cathodic protection, corrosion, coatings and linings, strength, lifecycle, alternative fuels, and steel water systems.

 API 653Tank Inspection & Repair

Industry context for inspection, repair, alteration, and reconstruction of aboveground storage tanks within its scope.

 API 2350Overfill Prevention

Industry context for overfill prevention systems and operating practices for petroleum storage tanks within its scope.

06 · LIFECYCLE & DECISION CONTINUITY

The next integrity decision depends on what the organization remembers.

Design basis, inspection history, corrosion rates, repairs, product service, coatings and linings, CP performance, settlement, operating changes, field findings, and verification become more valuable when they remain connected across time.
