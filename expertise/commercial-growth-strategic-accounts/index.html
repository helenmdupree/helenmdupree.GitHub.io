& Strategic Accounts

THE COMMERCIAL THESIS

You don’t walk in with a revenue strategy.
You build one.

A growth target is not a growth strategy. Before prescribing more sellers, a new market, higher prices, executive access, another channel, or a new offering, leadership needs to understand where profitable revenue can credibly come from and what is preventing capture today.

SOUBEL treats opinions as hypotheses, tests them against evidence, and builds the commercial strategy from what survives review.

DEEPER COMMERCIAL LOGIC

Where is growth actually being lost, missed, or blocked?

SOUBEL examines three different kinds of gaps because the same revenue symptom can have very different causes. The distinction matters before leadership commits money or people to a remedy.

01 · COMMERCIAL

Capability is not converting into profitable revenue.

Look for whitespace, stalled opportunities, low share of wallet, retention issues, pricing leakage, weak conversion, or underdeveloped cross-sell.

02 · COMPETITIVE

The market is rewarding someone or something else.

Test customer feedback, win/loss evidence, competitor behavior, demand patterns, buying triggers, and whether the offer is positioned around what customers actually value.

03 · OPERATIONAL

The organization cannot fully deliver the commercial promise.

Examine capacity, technical support, proposal cycle, qualifications, execution constraints, ownership, and other internal barriers that can suppress otherwise viable demand.

STRATEGIC ACCOUNTS

Account growth is more than executive access.

Strong account strategy connects customer priorities to the real buying system: who owns the problem, who owns the budget, who carries implementation risk, what proof is required, where whitespace exists, and whether the organization can deliver the promised outcome.

Questions the account strategy should answer

Priority: What operational or commercial outcome matters enough to fund?

Pathway: Who influences, approves, uses, blocks, and ultimately owns the decision?

Whitespace: Where is there a credible unmet need or under-positioned capability?

Proof: What evidence must survive scrutiny beyond the initial champion?

Economics: What makes action worth more than inaction?
