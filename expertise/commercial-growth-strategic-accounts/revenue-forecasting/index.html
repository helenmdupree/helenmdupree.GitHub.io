REVENUE FORECASTING

A forecast should explain where the number comes from.

Forecast confidence should rise when observable customer action, commercial agreement, technical qualification, decision timing, and delivery reality become more visible.

FORECAST CONFIDENCE

CommittedObservable customer action and commercial agreement support the timing.
ProbableMaterial evidence exists, with identifiable steps still required.
DevelopmentalThe opportunity is real, but customer commitment remains incomplete.
SpeculativePotential exists, but the evidence is not yet strong enough to carry the number.

FORECAST ARCHITECTURE

Make the assumptions visible.

SOUBEL approaches forecasting through the evidence beneath the number: opportunity stage, customer commitment, buying process, technical qualification, competitive position, pricing, partner involvement, delivery capacity, historical conversion, timing, and known dependencies.

The objective is not a more complicated forecast. It is a forecast leadership can interrogate, challenge, and manage.

A CRM probability percentage is not evidence of customer commitment.

FORECAST DISCIPLINE

Build confidence from what is actually known.

The forecast becomes more useful when evidence quality, revenue motion, classification, and reporting architecture remain visible.

EVIDENCE

Evidence before probability

Use customer behavior, buying milestones, technical qualification, pricing, competition, delivery constraints, and known dependencies to support forecast confidence instead of relying on a percentage alone.

REVENUE MOTION

Separate what behaves differently

Existing-account expansion, new-logo growth, channel revenue, recurring revenue, project-based sales, and one-time transactions do not carry the same predictability and should not be forecast as though they do.

CLASSIFICATION

Name what the business actually knows

Committed, Probable, Developmental, and Speculative revenue should carry different evidence burdens, management actions, and expectations.

INFORMATION TRUST

Trust the architecture before the report

Metric definitions, source systems, manual adjustments, dependencies, and decision use should remain connected so the forecast can be traced back to its underlying assumptions.
