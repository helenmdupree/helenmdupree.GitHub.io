REVENUE FORECASTING

A forecast should explain
where the number comes from.

A useful forecast distinguishes committed revenue from probable and developmental revenue, identifies what must happen for an opportunity to advance, and exposes where timing, evidence, customer action, or internal execution could change the outcome.

← Commercial Growth & Strategic Accounts

SOUBEL approaches forecasting through the underlying commercial evidence: opportunity stage, customer commitment, buying process, technical qualification, competitive position, pricing, partner involvement, delivery capacity, historical conversion, timing, and known dependencies.

A CRM probability percentage is not evidence of customer commitment. Forecast confidence should rise when observable customer actions, buying milestones, technical qualification, commercial agreement, and decision timing become visible.

FORECAST DISCIPLINE

Make the assumptions visible enough to manage.

01

Evidence before probability

Use customer behavior, buying milestones, technical qualification, pricing, competition, delivery constraints, and known dependencies to support forecast confidence instead of relying on a percentage alone.

02

Separate revenue motions

Existing-account expansion, new-logo growth, channel revenue, recurring revenue, project-based sales, and one-time transactions behave differently and should not be treated as though they carry the same predictability.

03

Classify what the business actually knows

Committed, Probable, Developmental, and Speculative revenue should carry different evidence burdens and management actions.

04

Trust the reporting architecture before trusting the report

Metric definitions, source systems, manual adjustments, dependencies, and decision use should remain connected. A metric earns trust when its definition, source, assumptions, and decision use remain visible.
