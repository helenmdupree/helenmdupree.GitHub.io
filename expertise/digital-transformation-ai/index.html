COMMERCIAL POSITIONING

Let’s be entirely honest: Companies aren’t looking to buy another piece of software.

They’re looking for the operational certainty that keeps their people safe, their assets running, and their obligations under control. In heavy industry, sophisticated technology still has to work in the real world — against real equipment, real operating conditions, real physics, and real consequences. If it doesn’t make the work clearer, the decisions faster, or the operation safer and more reliable, it becomes noise.

My focus is on relieving that operational pressure by connecting digital capability to the things the customer already cares about: protecting the asset, reducing risk, improving maintenance and uptime, supporting compliance, defending throughput, and making better use of capital.

Protect the AssetReduce the RiskProtect the Economics

The math has to work, but the execution rules. Commercial credibility comes from understanding where the data comes from, where uncertainty enters, how the technology changes field behavior, and whether the people responsible for the asset can trust the recommendation enough to act on it, safely.

EVERGREEN REVENUE

Create enough continuing value that the relationship naturally becomes recurring revenue.

Customer Value↓Trust + Adoption↓Scale↓Recurring Revenue

The goal is not to sell another subscription. It is to become valuable enough that the customer wants the relationship to continue.

THE INDUSTRIAL BUYING COMMITTEE

Six Different Definitions of “Yes”

A technically strong solution can still stall when operations, engineering, IT/OT, finance, executive leadership and procurement are solving for different risks. Enterprise adoption happens when those six definitions of “yes” converge around one credible decision.

Reality Validation Criteria

Each stakeholder is validating a different dimension of whether the solution can survive the real operating, technical, economic and commercial environment.

OPERATIONS

This will work in the field.

It cannot create unnecessary operational friction, downtime or a workflow people will resist using.

ENGINEERING / INTEGRITY

The technical basis is defensible.

The evidence, assumptions and methodology must withstand engineering scrutiny and applicable regulatory, API, ASME and AMPP requirements.

IT / OT / DATA

It can connect without creating another risk.

Architecture, cybersecurity, integration, data context, ownership and governance have to work in the existing environment.

FINANCE

The economics justify the change.

TCO, avoided cost, OPEX/CAPEX impact, risk reduction and the cost of doing nothing need to make sense.

EXECUTIVE SPONSOR / LEADERSHIP

Can the value proven here multiply at scale?

The first deployment should demonstrate measurable ROI, savings, risk reduction or operating improvement — and show how that value can expand across additional assets, sites or business units.

PROCUREMENT / COMMERCIAL

The company and the commercial model can survive enterprise scrutiny.

Vendor qualification, contracting, pricing, implementation obligations and commercial risk all have to work.

The sale moves when all six definitions of “yes” begin pointing toward the same decision.

PILOT TO SCALE

A pilot should prove more than the technology. It should prove the path to scale.

A pilot is a limited real-world deployment that lets the customer test a technology before committing to broader adoption. Commercially, it is the bridge from interest to commitment.

The objective is not simply to win the PILOT. Design it to prove what the customer needs to see to say yes to scale.

HOW I QUALIFY THE OPPORTUNITY

Before talking platform, I want to understand the decision.

01What operating problem is important enough to change? If the problem is vague, the value case will be vague too.
02What does the current state cost? Downtime, field labor, rework, lost throughput, risk exposure, engineering hours or delayed decisions create the economic baseline.
03What data and evidence exist today? The architecture, quality and accessibility of information can determine whether the proposed use case is viable.
04Who owns the outcome? A technical user, an operational owner and an economic buyer may all be different people.
05What must the pilot prove? Define the proof criteria before the technology is deployed.
06What would prevent adoption? Integration, trust, workflow friction, security, training, ownership and procurement can matter as much as model performance.
07What creates the expansion case? The first use case should show where repeatable value can extend across assets, sites or business units.
