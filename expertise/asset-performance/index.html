ASSET PERFORMANCE

Running ≠ Performing

Asset performance asks whether a physical asset is fulfilling its intended purpose safely, reliably, and economically. SOUBEL connects condition, integrity, reliability, operations, maintenance, data, risk, and economics to the decisions that determine whether an asset continues to create value.

Explore the perspectiveOpen the knowledge branch

THE SOUBEL PERSPECTIVE

Asset performance has no meaning without context.

The same equipment can be considered successful or underperforming depending on what the operation requires from it.

Throughput, reliability, integrity, energy use, maintenance burden, availability, remaining life, safety, and cost all shape the answer.

Asset performance begins by understanding the intended function and operating objective before deciding what data, technology, maintenance, or intervention matters.

THE PERFORMANCE QUESTION

What does the operation require from the asset?

Performance starts with the required operating outcome, not a generic benchmark. The question is whether the asset can continue to do the job the operation needs, under the conditions it actually faces, with an acceptable balance of reliability, risk, cost, and remaining life.

OPERATING REQUIREMENT

What result has to be delivered?

Duty, throughput, quality, availability, operating envelope, safety, and service expectations define what good performance means for that asset.

CONDITION & RELIABILITY

What is changing, and does it threaten that result?

Inspection, sensors, integrity evidence, process conditions, maintenance history, alarms, OEM information, and field observations help establish whether condition or reliability is moving away from what the operation requires.

OPERATING CONSEQUENCE

Why does the change matter now?

Production, safety, energy use, maintenance burden, workforce capacity, spares, operating windows, customer commitments, and business continuity determine the practical consequence of waiting or acting.

ECONOMICS & REMAINING LIFE

What response creates the strongest operating value?

Monitoring, maintenance, repair, derating, operating changes, additional evidence, replacement, and capital investment should be weighed against cost, risk, downtime, reversibility, and the useful life that remains.

DATA & DIGITAL SUPPORT

Technology should sharpen the decision, not become the story.

SCADA, historians, inspection systems, maintenance records, field data, OEM information, engineering models, digital twins, and AI can extend visibility and decision support. Their value depends on whether the information preserves operating context, can be trusted, and helps the organization make a better decision.

SOUBEL view: more data is not the objective. Better understanding of asset condition, consequence, timing, and choice is.

THE DECISION TEST

Does the information change what the organization should do?

That question keeps asset performance grounded in operating reality. Evidence matters when it helps the organization understand what is changing, why it matters, what options are credible, and which action best supports the required outcome.
