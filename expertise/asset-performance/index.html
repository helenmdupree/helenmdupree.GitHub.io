ASSET PERFORMANCE

An asset is not performing
simply because it is running.

Asset performance asks whether a physical asset is fulfilling its intended purpose safely, reliably, and economically. SOUBEL connects condition, integrity, reliability, operations, maintenance, data, risk, and economics to the decisions that determine whether an asset continues to create value.

Explore the perspectiveOpen the knowledge branch

THE SOUBEL PERSPECTIVE

Performance has no meaning without context.

The same equipment can be considered successful or underperforming depending on what the operation requires from it. Throughput, reliability, integrity, energy use, maintenance burden, availability, remaining life, safety, and cost all shape the answer. Asset performance begins by understanding the intended function and operating objective before deciding what data, technology, maintenance, or intervention matters.

THE PERFORMANCE SYSTEM

Condition is only one part of the performance question.

A useful asset-performance view keeps the physical asset, the operating environment, the maintenance strategy, risk, and economics connected. The objective is not to collect more information. It is to improve the quality and timing of the decisions made from it.

01

Operating Objective

Define what the asset is expected to accomplish, under what conditions, and which outcomes matter to the operation.

02

Condition & Health

Use inspection, monitoring, process history, field evidence, and maintenance records to understand what is changing.

03

Reliability & Maintenance

Connect failure modes, criticality, work history, and intervention timing to the reliability the operation requires.

04

Risk & Economics

Consider consequence, downtime, OPEX, capital, lost production, remaining life, and other business effects alongside technical condition.

05

Decision & Learning

Act, measure the outcome, preserve the evidence, and use what was learned to improve the next operating or maintenance decision.

FROM SIGNAL TO OUTCOME

Asset performance is a continuous decision system.

Condition & Context→Risk & Criticality→Decision→Intervention→Outcome & Learning

A sensor alarm, inspection result, reliability metric, maintenance event, or process deviation is evidence. Its value comes from understanding the operating context, deciding whether action is warranted, and learning whether the intervention improved the outcome.

ASSET HEALTH & CONDITION

Knowing what changed is not the same as knowing what it means.

Asset-health information can come from sensors, inspections, corrosion and integrity programs, process conditions, maintenance records, alarms, operator observations, OEM systems, and field history. The decision challenge is to separate signal from noise, understand what the change means for the intended function, and determine whether it changes priority or action.

RELIABILITY & MAINTENANCE

The maintenance question is not simply whether to act. It is when, where, and why.

Reliability and maintenance strategy translate asset condition and failure risk into planned work. Criticality, failure modes, operating consequence, workforce capacity, spares, maintenance windows, and cost all influence the timing and value of intervention. A better maintenance decision is one that is technically justified and operationally executable.

OPERATIONS & ECONOMICS

Uptime is not the whole value equation.

An asset can be available and still underperform. Throughput may be constrained, energy consumption may be excessive, degradation may be accelerating, maintenance cost may be rising, or remaining life may be consumed faster than expected. Asset-performance decisions become more useful when technical condition is considered alongside the economic and operational consequences of acting, waiting, repairing, replacing, derating, or changing how the asset is operated.

DATA & DIGITAL CONTEXT

Data has to become context before it becomes intelligence.

SCADA, historians, inspection systems, maintenance records, field data, OEM information, engineering models, and integrity platforms often describe different parts of the same asset. Asset performance improves when those sources can be placed in enough context to explain what is happening, what changed, and why the change matters.

Asset Data→Context→Insight→Decision→Action→Outcome
Where digital twins and AI enter the conversation: Digital models and AI can extend monitoring, pattern recognition, prediction, optimization, and decision support. Their value still depends on the quality of the underlying data, the operating context, validation, and the domain judgment used to interpret the result.
