Asset Integrity Management

AIM ACROSS THE ASSET LIFECYCLE

Integrity knowledge should grow with the asset.

From design intent and commissioning through operation, maintenance, modification, life extension, and decommissioning, Asset Integrity Management depends on carrying forward what the organization has learned about the asset.

LIFECYCLE CONTINUITY

Every stage creates evidence for the next.

Design basis, materials, construction records, commissioning data, operating history, inspection findings, repairs, modifications, and field verification each add to the organization’s understanding of the asset. Their value increases when that history remains connected over time.

Design Intent→Build & Commission→Operate & Maintain→Inspect & Assess→Modify or Extend Life→Decommission

THREE LIFECYCLE QUESTIONS

The integrity question changes as the asset changes.

ESTABLISH THE BASELINE

What was the asset designed, built, and commissioned to do?

Design intent, materials, fabrication and construction history, baseline condition, testing, commissioning records, and early operating information establish the starting point for future integrity decisions.

OPERATE, ASSESS & ADAPT

What has changed during service?

Operating conditions, maintenance, inspection, monitoring, degradation, repairs, field findings, and changing service demands build the condition history used to assess risk and determine action.

MODIFY, EXTEND OR RETIRE

What evidence supports the next lifecycle decision?

Modifications, changes in service, life-extension decisions, major repairs, replacement, and decommissioning should reflect both current condition and the history that produced it.

THE CONTINUITY RECORD

Asset history is more useful when the reasoning stays attached.

A lifecycle record gains value when it preserves more than dates and completed tasks. It should help future reviewers understand what changed, which evidence mattered, why decisions were made, what the field confirmed, and how the asset’s operating context evolved.

DESIGN & MATERIAL BASIS

Preserve the starting assumptions.

Materials, configuration, design conditions, construction details, testing, and commissioning information establish the technical context inherited by later decisions.

CONDITION & OPERATING HISTORY

Keep physical condition connected to service.

Inspection, monitoring, maintenance, process or operating changes, corrosion-control information, repairs, and field observations become more useful when viewed together.

CHANGE & DECISION HISTORY

Carry the basis for major changes forward.

Modifications, altered service, risk decisions, deferred work, mitigation, and life-extension choices should remain understandable after the people who made them have moved on.

VERIFICATION & LEARNING

Let completed work improve future decisions.

Field verification, closure evidence, lessons learned, and reassessment results should strengthen the next cycle rather than disappear into separate records.
