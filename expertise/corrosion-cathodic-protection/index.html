CORROSION & CATHODIC PROTECTION

Corrosion: How Can We Prevent It?

Corrosion is an amazing science lesson happening all around us.

Explore the scienceOpen the Knowledge Library

At its core is electrochemistry — the movement of electrons and ions that can slowly change metal when it interacts with its environment. Rust is one familiar example, but corrosion takes many forms.

What you may not have known is that there is an entire global industry built around controlling it.

Corrosion can be managed in many ways, including coatings, material selection, chemical treatment, monitoring, inspection, maintenance, and specialized electrical methods.

And one of the ways to control it is a method called CATHODIC PROTECTION.

HOW CATHODIC PROTECTION WORKS

Cathodic protection uses electrochemistry to reduce corrosion.

It changes the conditions at the surface of the metal being protected. On buried or submerged structures such as pipelines and tanks, that protection can be created using sacrificial anodes or an impressed-current system powered by an external DC source.

HOW DO WE KNOW IT IS WORKING?

Cathodic protection has to be measured.

Test stations, reference electrodes, field surveys, remote monitoring, and other tools give technicians access to the electrical conditions around a protected structure and help determine whether the system is performing as intended.
