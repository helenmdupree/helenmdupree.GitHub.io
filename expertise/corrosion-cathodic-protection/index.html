CORROSION & CATHODIC PROTECTION

Protection is a system,
not a product.

SOUBEL brings practical commercial fluency to corrosion and cathodic protection: understanding how materials, equipment, field execution, monitoring, data, and integrity requirements work together, and why those connections matter to the operator.

Explore the perspectiveOpen the knowledge branch

THE SOUBEL PERSPECTIVE

Technical fluency with a commercial lens.

SOUBEL does not position itself as an engineering design authority. The value is different: years spent working inside the corrosion-control market, translating technical capability into customer decisions, supporting field and channel execution, and understanding how the pieces of a CP program affect reliability, cost, evidence, and risk.

That experience matters because a technically sound component is only one part of a functioning corrosion-control system. Selection, installation, monitoring, maintenance, field competence, data quality, and operating context determine whether the intended protection is actually delivered.

WHAT MATTERS

Four connected parts of the corrosion-control conversation.

SOUBEL keeps the technical foundation visible without trying to reproduce the industry's encyclopedia. The focus is on the connections that affect decisions.

01

Protection & Criteria

Understand the purpose of cathodic protection, how protection is evaluated, and why measurements and criteria have to be interpreted in context rather than treated as isolated numbers.

02

Materials & Equipment

Anodes, rectifiers, reference electrodes, test stations, junction boxes, RMUs, sensors, cables, and related hardware form a system. The useful question is not simply what each item is, but how its selection and performance affect the whole.

03

Field Execution & Monitoring

Surveys, testing, interruption, troubleshooting, commissioning, maintenance, and remote monitoring turn design intent into operating evidence. Execution quality determines the confidence that can be placed in the result.

04

Integrity & Decision Context

CP sits inside a larger integrity system that includes coatings, interference, inspection, ECDA, regulatory requirements, maintenance priorities, and risk. Better decisions come from seeing those dependencies together.

FROM COMPONENT TO OUTCOME

The value is in understanding how the pieces connect.

Materials & equipment→Field execution→Monitoring & data→Integrity evidence→Operational decision

A rectifier, anode, RMU, survey, or data point has limited meaning by itself. Its value comes from the role it plays in producing reliable protection and defensible evidence about asset condition.

The corrosion-control plan defines the objective. Materials put it into practice.

Cathodic protection depends on more than a design calculation. Anodes, rectifiers, reference electrodes, test stations, junction boxes, RMUs, sensors, coupons, cables, and connections all contribute to how the system is installed, monitored, maintained, and trusted over time. SOUBEL brings practical experience with these materials and equipment and focuses on how their performance affects reliability, field execution, data confidence, and long-term asset integrity.

INTEGRITY DATA & SOFTWARE

The field measurement is only the beginning.

Field data becomes more valuable when it can be preserved, connected to asset history, evaluated over time, and placed in the context of other integrity information. Data collection systems and integrity software help move information from the technician in the field into records that support monitoring, compliance, analysis, prioritization, and operational decisions.

Field Equipment→Data Collection→Integrity Software→Analysis→Decision

Where AI enters the conversation: As CP surveys, remote-monitoring data, inspection findings, direct examinations, coating information, and asset history are brought together, software can support increasingly sophisticated analysis. The important question is not whether AI can process the data, but whether the underlying evidence, context, and validation are strong enough to support a trustworthy integrity decision.
