CORROSION & CATHODIC PROTECTION

Cathodic Protection Systems

Two approaches to applying cathodic protection: impressed current and galvanic systems.

Impressed Current Cathodic Protection

ICCP uses an external DC power source to drive and control protective current through the electrolyte to the structure. It is suited to applications with higher or variable current demand.

Galvanic / Sacrificial Cathodic Protection

Galvanic CP uses a more active metal anode, commonly magnesium or zinc, to supply protective current without an external power source. The sacrificial anode is gradually consumed while protecting the steel.

ANODE MATERIALS

Cathodic Protection Anodes

Technical Notes

Galvanic-anode potentials vary with alloy composition, electrolyte, temperature, surface condition, and operating conditions. Reference-electrode basis must be stated when comparing measured potentials.

ICCP systems use an external DC power source to drive current. ICCP anode materials are therefore compared by electrochemical performance, current-output capability, consumption, environment, configuration, and design life rather than by position on a galvanic nobility scale.

Mixed Metal Oxide (MMO) Anodes

MMO anodes typically consist of a titanium substrate coated with an electrocatalytic layer of mixed metal oxides. These coatings commonly incorporate iridium- and/or ruthenium-based oxides, with coating chemistry selected for the intended electrolyte and operating environment. The titanium provides the structural substrate, while the oxide coating provides the electrochemically active surface from which impressed current is discharged into the electrolyte.

Unlike galvanic anodes, MMO anodes do not provide the driving voltage for cathodic protection. An external DC power source supplies the driving force. This allows MMO anodes to operate at comparatively high current densities with very low anode consumption when properly selected and operated.

MMO-coated titanium is available in configurations including tubular, wire, ribbon, mesh, and discrete anodes. Applications include buried pipelines and groundbeds, tanks, marine structures, and other immersed systems. Coating chemistry and loading, anode configuration, electrolyte, operating current density, and required design life are important considerations in system design.
