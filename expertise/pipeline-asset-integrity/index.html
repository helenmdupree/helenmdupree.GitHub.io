PIPELINE & ASSET INTEGRITY

Integrity is not an inspection.
It is a decision system.

SOUBEL connects the evidence surrounding an asset — threats, inspection and assessment, field findings, remediation, records, risk, and operating context — to the decisions that protect reliability and reduce uncertainty.

Explore the integrity cycleOpen the knowledge branch

THE SOUBEL PERSPECTIVE

The finding matters. What happens next matters more.

Pipeline integrity brings together engineering, inspection, field execution, operations, regulation, data, and risk. SOUBEL's perspective is grounded in working around those disciplines and translating complex technical capability into customer, operational, and commercial decisions.

The value is not in pretending every discipline is the same. It is in understanding how they connect: what threat is being evaluated, what evidence supports the finding, what additional validation is needed, what action follows, and whether the resulting record can support the next decision.

THE INTEGRITY CYCLE

From potential threat to defensible action.

No single assessment method characterizes every integrity threat. The useful picture emerges when threat identification, appropriate assessment, field validation, remediation, and continuing evaluation remain connected.

01

Threat & Risk Context

Corrosion, cracking, manufacturing and construction issues, mechanical damage, geohazards, operations, and other threats do not carry the same likelihood or consequence. Integrity begins with understanding what can fail, where, and why it matters.

02

Assessment & Inspection

ILI, pressure testing, direct assessment, direct examination, NDE, and other methods provide different kinds of evidence. Method selection has to fit the threat and the information needed from the asset.

03

Validation & Remediation

Indications become more meaningful when they are validated against field condition. Integrity excavations, direct examination, metallurgical or laboratory analysis, repair, replacement, and mitigation turn assessment findings into physical action.

04

Records, Learning & Reassessment

The completed repair is not the end of the information chain. Findings, measurements, decisions, and outcomes become part of the asset record and inform risk models, future assessments, maintenance priorities, and reassessment.

FROM SIGNAL TO DECISION

Integrity evidence gains value when the chain stays connected.

Threat→Assessment→Validation→Remediation→Record & Reassessment

An ILI call, CP reading, direct-assessment indication, laboratory result, or excavation finding is evidence — not the entire decision. Confidence grows when the evidence can be reconciled with asset history, field condition, consequence, engineering judgment, and the action ultimately taken.

ASSESSMENT & VALIDATION

Inspection finds the signal. Validation establishes what is actually there.

Assessment technologies help operators identify and characterize potential integrity concerns, but the operational question is what the evidence means for the asset. That can require data integration, comparison with prior history, integrity excavations and direct examination, NDE, or laboratory and metallurgical work before repair and mitigation decisions are made.

INTEGRITY DATA & DECISION SUPPORT

The asset record should become more valuable with every assessment.

Integrity programs generate information across years, technologies, vendors, field activities, repairs, and operating conditions. The challenge is not simply storing more data. It is preserving enough context and traceability to understand what changed, why an action was taken, and what the outcome should teach the next assessment.

Inspection Data→Field Evidence→Integrated Record→Risk & Analysis→Decision

Where software and AI enter the conversation: Integrity software can help organize, correlate, visualize, and analyze information across the asset lifecycle. AI can extend that capability, but only when data quality, context, validation, and human judgment are strong enough for the resulting analysis to deserve operational trust.

REMEDIATION & FOLLOW-THROUGH

Evidence drives the decision.

Integrity management ultimately has to produce action: evaluate the condition, prioritize it appropriately, repair or mitigate when required, document what was found and done, and carry that learning forward. This is where inspection programs, field execution, engineering decisions, maintenance planning, compliance, and budget meet.
