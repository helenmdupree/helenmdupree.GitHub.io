# Physics-Based Digital Tools — Research Notes

Saved for future Digital & AI Knowledge Library copy development.

## Core idea
Digital tools used in heavy industry can be physics-based.  They are increasingly important for modeling real-world constraints such as fluid dynamics, heat transfer, structural stress, machinery behavior, and other physical operating conditions.

## Topics to develop
- **Digital twins:** Some platforms combine 1D system simulation and 3D engineering analysis to represent physical machinery and evaluate behavior under operating conditions.
- **Physics-grounded AI:** Some approaches embed physical laws and constraints — such as thermodynamics and mechanics — into machine-learning models rather than relying only on statistical extrapolation.
- **Core simulation methods:** Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) can model fluid, gas and heat-flow behavior.  Discrete Element Modeling (DEM) can model bulk-material movement, contact behavior and equipment wear.
- Important editorial distinction: industrial digital systems may be statistical, physics-based, hybrid, or data-driven.  Avoid describing all industrial digital technology as “just math.”

## Commercial-page language informed by this note
“In heavy industry, sophisticated technology still has to work in the real world — against real equipment, real operating conditions, real physics, and real consequences.”
