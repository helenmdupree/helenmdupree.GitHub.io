01 · DIGITAL TWINS & SIMULATION

What is a digital twin?

A digital twin is a digital representation of a physical asset, system or process that is connected to information about the real thing. Depending on the application, that information may update continuously, periodically, or when a specific event occurs. The purpose is not simply to visualize an asset, but to understand condition and behavior, test scenarios, and support decisions before changing the physical system.

Physical AssetOperating EvidenceDigital RepresentationAnalysis / SimulationDecision
SEE · What feeds it

Sensors, SCADA and historians can provide operating measurements. Inspection records, engineering data, maintenance history and asset context add evidence that live telemetry alone cannot provide.

UNDERSTAND · What it does

The twin organizes that evidence around a representation of the asset so teams can monitor condition, compare expected and observed behavior, simulate scenarios, and anticipate possible outcomes.

ACT · What changes

Engineers, operators and asset teams use the result to support inspection, maintenance, operating or planning decisions in the physical world. The twin informs the decision; it does not replace engineering judgment.

FOUR COMMON LEVELSComponent — one part or piece of equipment.Asset — interacting components considered as one asset.System — multiple assets and the relationships between them.Process — a broader operating process, facility or production system.
WHAT MAKES IT A TWIN?

A 3D model is not automatically a digital twin. Neither is a dashboard showing live telemetry. A useful twin connects the physical system, its evidence and context, a digital representation, and analysis or simulation to a decision the organization needs to make.

INDUSTRIAL EXAMPLE · COMPRESSOR STATION
Pressure · temperature · vibration · flowInspection + maintenance historyAsset model + operating contextCondition / scenario analysisOperate · inspect · maintain · plan
THE LIMIT

The twin inherits the evidence underneath it. Missing inspection history, poorly contextualized sensor data, incorrect asset records or uncertain assumptions do not disappear inside a sophisticated model. They can create false confidence faster.

02 · AI / ML & ANALYTICS

How does a system learn from operating data?

Analytics, machine learning and artificial intelligence overlap, but they are not interchangeable. Analytics examines data to describe, compare and explain what is happening. Machine learning uses examples in data to learn patterns that can classify, estimate or predict. AI is the broader field: it can use machine learning and other methods to interpret information, recognize patterns and support decisions.

Operating EvidencePrepare + ContextualizeAnalyze / LearnPattern / PredictionDecision Support
ANALYTICS · Examine

Summarizes and compares measurements, events and trends. It can show what happened, what changed and where relationships appear in the available evidence.

MACHINE LEARNING · Learn patterns

Uses historical examples to learn relationships without programming every rule individually. The learned model can then classify, estimate, forecast or flag new observations.

AI · Apply capability

Uses computational methods — which may include machine learning — to perform tasks such as recognition, prediction, language interpretation and decision support.

WHAT GOES INOperating data — pressure, temperature, flow, vibration and other measurements.Events — alarms, failures, trips and operating-state changes.Asset evidence — inspection, maintenance and condition history.Context — asset identity, time, location, units and operating conditions.
WHAT COMES OUT

Depending on the problem, the result may be a trend, anomaly, classification, forecast, estimated condition, ranked risk or other decision-support signal. A prediction is not automatically a decision; the operating consequence still has to be interpreted.

WHERE INDUSTRY USES IT
Pipeline IntegrityILI + CP + inspection + operating history→Threat patterns + condition / risk prioritization→Inspect · assess · intervene
Leak / Anomaly DetectionPressure + flow + acoustic / sensor evidence→Abnormal behavior detection→Investigate · isolate · respond
Process & Equipment ReliabilityVibration + temperature + process variables→Condition / anomaly signal→Inspect · maintain · adjust
Visual InspectionCamera + drone + inspection imagery→Detected condition / anomaly→Verify · inspect · respond
INDUSTRIAL EXAMPLE · ROTATING EQUIPMENT
Vibration · temperature · loadOperating + maintenance historyPattern / anomaly analysisCondition or failure-risk signalInspect · monitor · maintain
WHAT IT IS NOT

A dashboard is not automatically AI. A threshold alarm is not automatically machine learning. And AI / ML does not automatically mean automation: AI can recognize, estimate, predict or recommend; automation executes defined actions; agentic AI can coordinate multistep work toward a goal. A confident-looking prediction still has not proved that the underlying data represents the real operating condition correctly.

THE LIMIT

The model learns from the evidence it receives. Missing conditions, biased historical examples, bad labels, sensor problems, changing operating regimes or weak asset context can all distort what it learns. Performance therefore has to be tested against the operating environment where the result will actually be used.

03 · AGENTIC AI

What makes AI agentic?

Agentic AI moves beyond producing a single answer. An agent works toward a defined objective by gathering information, planning steps, using approved tools, evaluating results and deciding what to do next within boundaries established by people. In industrial environments, the important question is not whether an agent can act — it is what evidence it can access, what authority it has, and where human judgment must remain in control.

SENSE / TRIGGERAn event, condition or defined objective starts the work.GATHER CONTEXTRetrieve the evidence needed to understand the situation.REASON + PLANDetermine the next steps within the assigned objective.USE APPROVED TOOLSInteract only with systems and actions the agent is permitted to use.ACT / ESCALATEComplete permitted work or hand the decision to a person.
OBSERVE · Gather context

The agent retrieves permitted information from documents, databases, work systems or other approved sources needed to understand the task.

COORDINATE · Work the steps

It can plan a sequence, call tools, compare results, create intermediate outputs and adapt the next step rather than waiting for a new prompt after every action.

GOVERN · Bound the authority

Permissions, approval gates, escalation rules, logging and stop conditions determine which actions are automatic, which require review and which remain human-executed.

WHAT AN AGENT NEEDSObjective — a defined task or outcome rather than an open-ended instruction.Context — trusted information relevant to the asset, process or decision.Tools — explicitly permitted systems, APIs, search, calculations or workflow actions.Memory / state — enough retained task context to coordinate work across multiple steps.
WHAT MAKES IT DIFFERENT?

A chatbot responds. A workflow follows a predefined path. An agent can determine intermediate steps toward a goal and use tools to carry them out. That flexibility is useful — and it is exactly why authority, traceability and intervention points matter.

WHERE INDUSTRY COULD USE IT
Integrity ReviewInspection + ILI + CP + asset records→Assemble evidence + flag conflicts / gaps→Engineer reviews
Regulatory ChangeAgency rules + standards + internal procedures→Identify changes + affected requirements→Compliance validates
Maintenance CoordinationCondition or anomaly detectedGather asset + maintenance historyPrepare work order, parts + schedulingApprove or execute within authority
Abnormal Situation SupportAlarms + operating context + procedures→Retrieve + organize relevant evidence→Operator decides
AUTHORITY SHOULD FOLLOW CONSEQUENCE
LOW CONSEQUENCERetrieve · summarize · organize · draftREVIEW REQUIREDRecommend · prepare workflow · propose actionHUMAN CONTROLSafety-critical · irreversible · regulatory or operating decisions
WHAT IT IS NOT

Agentic does not mean unrestricted autonomy. Giving a model access to tools does not make its reasoning correct, and adding more agents does not automatically make a system more capable. In a consequential operating environment, an agent should have only the information and authority required for its task.

THE LIMIT

Agents can compound errors across multiple steps. Weak source information, incorrect retrieval, excessive permissions, ambiguous goals or an unverified intermediate result can propagate into later actions. Useful industrial agentic systems therefore need observable decision trails, constrained tool access, explicit escalation paths and human control where consequences are high.

04 · WORKFLOW AUTOMATION

Automation is not the same thing as AI.

Workflow automation moves repeatable work through defined rules, triggers, approvals and system integrations. Its strength is consistency: when the process and decision points are known, automation can reduce manual entry, waiting, missed handoffs and administrative variation without pretending that every decision should be made by a model.

Trigger / EventApply RulesRoute WorkApprove / EscalateRecord + Complete
TRIGGER · Start the process

A condition, form, inspection result, alarm, schedule or status change initiates a defined workflow.

ROUTE · Move the work

Rules determine what information is required, which system or person receives it, and where approvals or exceptions interrupt the normal path.

RECORD · Preserve evidence

Completed steps, timestamps, approvals, exceptions and resulting records create traceability instead of leaving the process buried in email and spreadsheets.

GOOD CANDIDATES FOR AUTOMATIONRepeatable — the process follows a recognizable sequence.Rule-based — routing and decision points can be defined explicitly.High-volume — repeated manual handling creates delay or inconsistency.Traceable — the organization benefits from knowing who did what, when and why.
WHERE AUTOMATION SHOULD STOP

Not every process should be automated end to end. Unusual field conditions, conflicting evidence, safety consequences, engineering judgment and regulatory interpretation may require a person to assess the situation rather than forcing it through the normal path.

WHERE INDUSTRY USES IT
Asset Integrity FindingsInspection finding · required review · assignment · remediation tracking · closure evidence
Operator QualificationCovered task · qualification requirements · worker verification · assignment or exception
Permit to WorkWork request · required reviews · authorization · execution · closeout
Management of ChangeProposed change · technical and safety review · approvals · implementation · documentation
WHAT IT IS NOT

Automation does not require artificial intelligence. A deterministic workflow can be highly valuable precisely because its rules and approval points are explicit. AI may help classify documents, summarize evidence or recommend a route, while the workflow remains responsible for controlling how the work moves.

THE LIMIT

Automating a weak process makes the weakness move faster. Poorly designed rules, missing exception paths, bad master data or unnecessary approvals can scale delay and error just as efficiently as a good workflow scales consistency. The process has to be understood before it is automated.

05 · INDUSTRIAL DATA & CONTEXT

How does industrial data become usable across an operating environment?

Industrial data is created by systems built for different jobs: control systems monitor the process, historians preserve time-series data, inspection systems document condition, maintenance systems track work, and engineering records describe how assets were designed and changed. Making those sources available in one place is only the beginning. The information also has to be connected to the correct physical asset, time, location, operating state and source so people and digital systems can interpret it correctly.

INDUSTRIAL DATA COMES FROM MANY PLACES
SCADA · PLCs · historians · sensorsILI · inspection · CP · field recordsCMMS / EAM · maintenance historyP&IDs · GIS · engineering documentsLaboratory · enterprise · regulatory records
IDENTIFY · What is it?

Asset identifiers and hierarchies connect a tag, reading or record to the correct system, facility, unit, asset, component and measurement point.

CONTEXTUALIZE · What does it mean?

Timestamps, units, location, operating state, measurement method and related asset information establish the conditions under which the data should be interpreted.

TRACE · Where did it come from?

Source and provenance preserve where information originated, how it was collected and what transformations occurred before another person, model or system used it.

CONNECTED DOES NOT AUTOMATICALLY MEAN UNDERSTOOD
INTEGRATIONCan the systems exchange the data?CONTEXTUALIZATIONCan another person or system understand what the data represents?PROVENANCECan we establish where it came from and what happened to it?
THE CONTEXT THAT TRAVELS WITH THE DATAAsset — which physical asset, component or measurement point does this belong to?Time & state — when was it captured, and what was the asset doing?Location — where on the asset or system does the evidence apply?Method & units — how was it measured, recorded or calculated?Relationships — what inspection, maintenance, engineering or operating records belong with it?
WHY THE RELATIONSHIPS MATTER

A larger data set is not necessarily better evidence. Information from different systems becomes useful together only when records are correctly aligned to the same asset, location and period — while preserving the conditions and methods under which each source was created.

INDUSTRIAL EXAMPLE · ONE NUMBER, THEN CONTEXT
0.25 inches per second (in/s)A vibration reading by itself.
Which pump and measurement point?What operating load and process condition?What is the normal baseline for this asset?When and how was the measurement collected?What do inspection and maintenance records show?

Once those relationships are established, the reading can be interpreted as evidence about a specific asset under specific conditions rather than treated as an isolated number.

THE LIMIT

Context cannot repair evidence that was never collected or make an unreliable source trustworthy. Incorrect asset mappings, inconsistent naming, unit conversions, timestamp problems, missing history and undocumented transformations can make connected data look coherent while quietly changing its meaning.

06 · CONNECTED OPERATIONS

How does information move from the field to the people who need it?

Connected operations links physical assets, field activity, operational systems and business applications so useful information can move beyond the system where it was created. The goal is not to make every asset autonomous. It is to give operators, engineers, inspectors and managers better visibility without erasing the boundaries between monitoring, coordination and control.

FROM THE PHYSICAL OPERATION TO OPERATIONAL USE
FIELD & ASSETSensors · instruments · PLCs / RTUs · cameras · inspection and mobile toolsEDGE, CONTROL & COMMUNICATIONSLocal control · gateways · edge processing · industrial networks and communicationsINFORMATION & CONTEXTSCADA · historians · asset frameworks · contextualized and integrated informationHUMAN & OPERATIONAL APPLICATIONSControl-room visibility · integrity · CMMS / EAM · work management · compliance
SEE · Extend visibility

Remote assets, secondary equipment and field activity can become visible beyond the immediate worksite through instrumentation, communications, mobile tools and connected applications.

CONNECT · Share information

Different systems can exchange authorized information without requiring every application to become the system of record or every operational environment to be controlled from one place.

USE · Put it into work

Connected information can support monitoring, integrity review, maintenance planning, field coordination, compliance reporting and other operating decisions.

OT AND IT HAVE DIFFERENT JOBS
OT · PHYSICAL OPERATIONMonitors and controls industrial processes and equipment. Safety, availability, timing and reliable operation are central concerns.IT · INFORMATION & BUSINESSSupports enterprise applications, information management, collaboration, reporting and business workflows.CONNECTED OPERATIONS · THE BRIDGEAllows useful information to move between operational and enterprise environments while preserving appropriate boundaries and authority.
WHAT THIS LOOKS LIKE IN PRACTICE
Field InspectionInspection evidence captured in the field becomes available with the correct asset, location and history for integrity review.
Remote Asset VisibilityOperating and condition information from remote stations or equipment reaches authorized operations and reliability teams without requiring someone to be physically present.
THE LIMIT

More connectivity also creates more dependencies. Communications can fail, field devices can lose power, timestamps and asset mappings can be wrong, and connected systems can expose information beyond its intended boundary if access is poorly designed. A connected operation still needs local resilience, clear ownership and deliberate control over what information — and what authority — crosses each boundary.

07 · EDGE COMPUTING & EDGE AI

Where does the intelligence run?

Edge computing moves selected processing closer to the equipment and data that need it rather than sending everything to a distant cloud or enterprise system first. Edge AI runs AI / ML inference — and, in some architectures, learning functions — on or near the systems generating the data. In industrial operations, the architecture decision is about what must happen near the asset for speed, resilience or data efficiency — and what is better handled upstream for broader analysis, governance and scale.

Device / SensorLocal Edge ProcessingAnalytics / AIAlert / Engineered ResponseSelected Results Upstream
RESPOND · Reduce latency

Local processing can evaluate time-sensitive operating data without waiting for a round trip to a remote platform before producing an alert, inference or permitted response.

RESILIENT · Keep working locally

Remote or bandwidth-constrained sites can continue selected monitoring and processing when connectivity is slow, intermittent or temporarily unavailable.

FILTER · Move what is useful

Edge systems can aggregate, compress or select relevant results so every raw signal does not have to travel upstream continuously.

WHAT BELONGS WHERE?At the edge — local acquisition, filtering, event detection, low-latency analytics, model inference and appropriately engineered local alerts, recommendations or automated responses.Upstream / cloud — heavier computation, fleet-level analysis, model development, enterprise integration, governance and cross-asset optimization.Across both — cybersecurity, identity, version control, data context, synchronization and lifecycle management.
THE ARCHITECTURE QUESTION

Edge is not simply another device category. It is a decision about where computation should occur. The right placement depends on latency, connectivity, cybersecurity, data volume, resilience, interoperability and where operating authority must remain.

HOW EDGE FITS INTO THE INDUSTRIAL DIGITAL STACK
PHYSICAL OPERATIONEquipment · sensors · instruments · PLCs / RTUsEDGE LAYERGateways · industrial computers · local analytics · AI inferenceCONNECTIVITYIndustrial networks · cellular · satellite · other communicationsPLATFORM / ENTERPRISECloud · historians · applications · fleet analytics · governance
WHAT THIS LOOKS LIKE IN PRACTICE
Remote Equipment MonitoringLocal data is evaluated near the asset; significant conditions and selected results are sent upstream for wider visibility and analysis.
Computer VisionA camera and local compute can run an approved model near the operation, sending detections or exceptions rather than continuously transporting every frame.
Intermittent ConnectivitySelected monitoring and processing continue locally while the site is disconnected, then synchronize permitted information when communications return.
WHAT IT IS NOT

Edge is not the same thing as a sensor, PLC, network, cloud platform or autonomous control system. Those components may participate in an edge architecture, but edge describes where selected computation and intelligence run relative to the physical operation.

THE LIMIT

Moving computation closer to the asset does not remove infrastructure or governance requirements. Edge systems still need secure deployment, model and software updates, device management, reliable time and asset context, interoperability, observability and clear authority over what can happen locally when upstream systems are unavailable.

08 · INDUSTRIAL CYBERSECURITY & OT SECURITY

How do you secure connected operational systems without compromising the operation?

Industrial cybersecurity protects the systems that monitor and control physical operations while preserving the availability, safety and reliability those operations depend on. As OT connects with enterprise applications, remote users, cloud services and edge systems, the security question becomes more than protecting data. It also means controlling who and what can reach operational assets, what authority crosses each boundary, and how the operation continues safely when something goes wrong.

Physical ProcessControl EnvironmentOT NetworkControlled BoundaryEnterprise / Cloud
KNOW · See the environment

Asset inventory, network visibility and system ownership establish what devices, software, communications paths and dependencies actually exist before controls are designed around them.

SEPARATE · Control the pathways

Segmentation, zones and controlled conduits limit unnecessary communication between systems and reduce how far an intrusion, mistake or compromised account can travel.

AUTHORIZE · Bound access

Identity, least-privilege access, secure remote connectivity and approval controls determine who can enter an operational environment and what they are permitted to do there.

WHY OT SECURITY IS DIFFERENTAvailability — an unavailable control or monitoring system can interrupt a physical operation.Safety — cyber events can have consequences beyond information loss when digital systems interact with equipment and process conditions.Legacy reality — long-lived assets may use older operating systems, protocols or equipment that cannot be patched or replaced like ordinary IT.Change discipline — updates, scans and security controls have to be evaluated against operating risk, compatibility and approved maintenance windows.
IT AND OT SHARE SECURITY GOALS, NOT IDENTICAL OPERATING CONDITIONS

Both environments need identity, access control, monitoring, vulnerability management and recovery. OT applies those practices where deterministic behavior, uptime, equipment compatibility, safety systems and physical consequences can constrain how and when security changes are made.

SECURITY ACROSS THE INDUSTRIAL DIGITAL STACK
PHYSICAL & CONTROLPLCs / RTUs · instruments · HMIs · safety and control systemsOT NETWORKIndustrial communications · segmentation · monitoring · protected pathwaysCONTROLLED BOUNDARYFirewalls · jump hosts · secure remote access · identity and authorizationENTERPRISE / CLOUDBusiness systems · analytics · cloud services · governance and security operations
WHAT THIS LOOKS LIKE IN PRACTICE
Vendor Remote AccessA technician receives authenticated, time-bounded access through an approved pathway rather than a permanent direct connection into the control environment.
Network SegmentationSystems are grouped by function and risk, with only required communications permitted across controlled boundaries.
Legacy Asset ProtectionWhen a device cannot be patched safely or promptly, compensating controls can reduce exposure while replacement or remediation is planned.
Detection & ResponseOperational monitoring identifies unusual communications or access while response procedures account for the need to preserve safe operation.
SECURITY SHOULD PRESERVE OPERATIONAL AUTHORITY
IDENTITYKnow the person, device or service requesting access.PERMISSIONAllow only the systems and actions required for the task.BOUNDARYControl where that access can travel.
WHAT IT IS NOT

OT security is not simply installing enterprise cybersecurity tools inside a plant or field network. A control that is appropriate for office IT can create operational risk if it disrupts timing, compatibility, availability or safety. Security architecture has to respect how the physical process actually operates.

THE LIMIT

No architecture eliminates cyber risk. Unknown assets, unsupported equipment, excessive privileges, insecure remote access, supply-chain dependencies, weak recovery planning and poorly controlled changes can undermine otherwise strong controls. Industrial cybersecurity therefore depends on sustained asset knowledge, disciplined access, monitored boundaries, tested recovery and coordination between operations, engineering, IT and security teams.
