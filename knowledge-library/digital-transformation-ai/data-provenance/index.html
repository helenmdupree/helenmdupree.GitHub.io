DATA PROVENANCE

A model can inherit a number without inheriting its meaning.

Industrial data becomes more trustworthy when the context behind the measurement remains attached to it: who collected it, where and when it was collected, what instrument and procedure were used, what conditions existed, and what quality limitations remain.

Why provenance mattersBack to Digital & AI Knowledge

WHY IT MATTERS

Data lineage is part of the evidence, not administrative decoration.

A cathodic-protection reading, inspection result, sensor value, maintenance record, laboratory result, or field observation can move through several systems before it reaches an analyst, model, dashboard, or digital twin. During that movement, important context can be lost. A timestamp may remain while the procedure version disappears. A value may retain units while the calibration status of the instrument is no longer visible. A location may be preserved while the operating conditions that shaped the reading are separated from it.

That matters because industrial AI often treats available data as input. The model can process the value whether or not the organization can still explain how the value was produced. The SOUBEL research review repeatedly returned to this problem: data confidence depends not only on the recorded measurement but on provenance information such as source, calibration, personnel qualification, procedure, environmental conditions, and quality flags.

The practical implication is straightforward. If a consequential decision depends on model output, the organization should be able to trace important inputs far enough back to understand their origin, limitations, and relevance. Provenance does not guarantee that the data are correct. It gives qualified people the information needed to judge how much confidence the data deserve.

PRACTICAL QUESTIONS

What should travel with important industrial data?

Source: What system, instrument, inspection, survey, document, or person created the information?
Identity: Is the correct asset, location, component, test point, or inspection segment unambiguous?
Time and operating condition: When was the information collected, and what was the asset doing at the time?
Method: What procedure, configuration, technique, or model version produced the result?
Quality: Was calibration, validation, completeness, or another quality check documented?
Change history: Can the organization tell whether the data, procedure, asset configuration, or interpretation changed later?

SOUBEL practical interpretation: These questions organize provenance themes identified in the research inventory. They are not presented as a new standard or proprietary framework.

RESEARCH FOUNDATION

Established requirements and an emerging digital trust problem.

The SOUBEL research inventory reviewed AMPP SP0169-2024, PHMSA requirements in 49 CFR Parts 192 and 195, Operator Qualification expectations, recent pipeline digital-twin literature, and related work on uncertainty and interoperability. The review found strong existing expectations around qualified personnel, procedures, records, and technical criteria, while also identifying continued industry attention to the problem of preserving sufficient context and uncertainty through digital systems.

The research exercise originally explored whether a formal provenance framework for pipeline digital twins could become a novel academic contribution. Novelty was not established. The useful outcome for practice is the clearer recognition that digital systems should preserve enough evidence about important inputs for people to evaluate their trustworthiness later.

Reference foundation: AMPP SP0169-2024 · PHMSA 49 CFR Parts 192/195 · PHMSA Operator Qualification requirements · pipeline digital-twin literature and data-confidence themes inventoried in SOUBEL research.

THE DECISION TEST

Can someone explain where the critical input came from and what could have affected it?

If not, the model may still produce an answer, but the organization has less evidence for deciding how much trust to place in that answer.
