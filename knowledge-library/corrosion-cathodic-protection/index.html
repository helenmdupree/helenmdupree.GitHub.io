CONNECTED CORROSION KNOWLEDGE

Four parts of the system. One integrity picture.

These guides connect cathodic protection fundamentals, materials and equipment, field execution, monitoring, and integrity requirements. Each section focuses on what the subject means in practice, why it matters to the asset, and where authoritative industry resources can provide additional technical depth.

01 · FOUNDATION

CP Fundamentals & Criteria

Cathodic protection controls corrosion by making the protected metal surface the cathode of an electrochemical cell. The useful operating question is whether the system is providing adequate protection under actual field conditions and whether the measurements used to demonstrate that protection are trustworthy.

Why it matters: Criteria, measurement technique, polarization, interference, and field conditions influence what a reading actually means.

02 · SYSTEM

Materials & Equipment

Galvanic and impressed-current systems rely on combinations of anodes, rectifiers, reference electrodes, test stations, junction boxes, coupons, cables, connections, RMUs, sensors, and related equipment. SOUBEL treats them as parts of one operating system rather than a product catalog.

Why it matters: Component selection, compatibility, installation, maintainability, monitoring capability, and lifecycle performance affect the reliability of the protection system and the economics of keeping it effective.

03 · EXECUTION

Field Execution & Monitoring

Close-interval surveys, test-station readings, interruption, commissioning, troubleshooting, periodic monitoring, and remote monitoring generate evidence about system performance. The technology can increase visibility, but the quality of installation, procedure, timing, and interpretation still matters.

Why it matters: More data is not automatically better evidence. Operators need measurements they can understand, trace, and use.

CIPS orientation · AMPP →
04 · CONTEXT

Integrity & Regulation

CP does not operate in isolation. Coating condition, electrical isolation, interference currents, test stations, monitoring, ECDA, inspection findings, maintenance, and regulatory requirements all shape the integrity decision.

Why it matters: A defensible corrosion-control decision connects the measurement to the condition of the asset, the integrity process, and the applicable requirements.
