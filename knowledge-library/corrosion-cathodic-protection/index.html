SOUBEL KNOWLEDGE LIBRARY · CORROSION & CATHODIC PROTECTION

Understand the protection system.
Follow the evidence.

Cathodic protection is not a row of products. It is a corrosion-control system whose materials, installation, measurements, monitoring, field practices, and integrity context have to work together.

Back to ExpertiseMaterials & Equipment

A PRACTICAL READER PATH

From protection objective to defensible evidence.

The reader should be able to answer four questions: What is the system trying to accomplish? What physical equipment makes that possible? What field evidence shows whether it is working? How does that evidence connect to the larger integrity decision?

Protection Objective→System & Materials→Field Evidence→Integrity Context→Decision

01 · FOUNDATION

CP fundamentals and criteria are about meaning, not just numbers.

Cathodic protection controls corrosion by shifting the electrochemical condition of the protected structure. In practice, the important question is whether the system is providing the intended protection under actual field conditions and whether the measurements used to demonstrate that protection are trustworthy.

WHAT HAS TO BE UNDERSTOOD

Potential, polarization, current, and environment.

Readings are influenced by reference-electrode condition, soil or electrolyte conditions, current distribution, coating condition, interruption method, electrical continuity, and interference. A number without context can be precise and still be misleading.

WHAT THE DECISION REQUIRES

Measurement technique and interpretation belong together.

The operating record should make clear what was measured, how it was measured, what criterion or engineering basis was applied, what conditions were present, and what action followed.

02 · MATERIALS & EQUIPMENT

The protection system is only as useful as the way its pieces work together.

The equipment below is not presented as a product catalog. Each item has a job in creating, distributing, measuring, monitoring, or preserving the evidence needed to manage the corrosion-control system.

CURRENT SOURCE

Anodes

Galvanic or impressed-current anodes provide the protective current that shifts the structure toward the intended electrochemical condition. Selection depends on environment, current demand, design life, installation geometry, backfill or electrolyte conditions, and compatibility with the rest of the system.

Decision questions: Is current output appropriate? Is the anode bed performing as expected? Is deterioration, depletion, shielding, or installation condition changing performance?

POWER & CONTROL

Rectifiers

Rectifiers provide controlled DC power for impressed-current systems. Output, tap settings, AC supply, enclosure condition, grounding, surge exposure, ventilation, connections, and maintenance history all affect reliable operation.

Decision questions: Is the rectifier producing the required output? Has output drifted? Are failures electrical, environmental, load-related, or caused elsewhere in the CP circuit?

MEASUREMENT

Reference Electrodes

Reference electrodes provide the stable comparison point used to measure structure-to-electrolyte potential. Condition, placement, contamination, contact with the electrolyte, temperature, and aging can affect confidence in the reading.

Decision questions: Can the reference be trusted? Is it placed where the measurement is meaningful? Does the reading agree with other evidence?

FIELD ACCESS

Test Stations

Test stations provide access to structure leads, coupons, bonds, casings, foreign lines, and other measurement points. Their usefulness depends on labeling, wiring integrity, accessibility, condition, and the ability to reconstruct what each lead represents.

Decision questions: Can a technician safely obtain the intended measurement? Are leads intact and correctly identified? Does the station preserve enough context for the next person?

CONNECTION & CONTROL

Junction Boxes

Junction boxes organize circuits, bonds, resistors, shunts, drainage connections, and monitoring points. Internal corrosion, loose connections, mislabeling, moisture intrusion, and undocumented changes can quietly undermine system performance.

Decision questions: Are connections electrically sound and documented? Can current paths be understood and adjusted without guesswork?

REMOTE VISIBILITY

Remote Monitoring Units

RMUs can provide recurring visibility into rectifier operation, potentials, current, interruption status, alarms, and other field conditions. Their value depends on sensor quality, communications, configuration, data validation, alarm logic, and a defined response process.

Decision questions: What condition is being monitored? What change should trigger action? Who owns the response when the signal moves outside expectation?

CONDITION EVIDENCE

Sensors

Sensors can extend visibility into AC current, corrosion rate, environmental condition, current density, or other parameters that affect integrity. The useful question is not how much data can be collected, but which measurement changes a decision.

Decision questions: Is the sensor measuring the right variable at the right location and frequency? How is it validated?

LOCAL CONDITION

Coupons

Coupons can provide localized information about polarization, current density, AC effects, or exposure conditions when designed and interpreted for the intended purpose. Placement and electrical configuration are central to what the result means.

Decision questions: What question is the coupon intended to answer? Is its exposure representative? Is the interpretation consistent with other field evidence?

SYSTEM CONTINUITY

Cables & Connections

Cables, splices, headers, structure leads, bonds, and terminations carry the current and measurements that make the system function. Mechanical damage, coating failure, poor splices, corrosion, resistance, and undocumented repairs can create failures that look like problems somewhere else.

Decision questions: Is continuity intact? Are connections protected and maintainable? Can the circuit be traced from source to structure and back to the record?

03 · FIELD EVIDENCE

Field practice determines whether the data deserves confidence.

Corrosion-control decisions depend on repeatable measurements, correct procedures, qualified field execution, and enough context to understand what changed.

SURVEY

CIPS / CIS

Close-interval potential surveys increase spatial resolution along a structure. Meaningful interpretation depends on synchronized interruption where appropriate, accurate stationing, reference-electrode control, survey timing, and comparison with prior or complementary data.

COATING EVIDENCE

DCVG and coating surveys

Coating surveys help locate and characterize coating discontinuities so CP and coating condition can be considered together. Survey results become more useful when they support prioritization and are reconciled with excavation findings.

CURRENT PATHS

Interference

Foreign CP systems, bonds, crossings, grounding systems, DC sources, and other current paths can change potentials and current distribution. Interference work requires understanding where current enters, leaves, and how mitigation affects both structures.

AC EXPOSURE

AC interference & mitigation

Parallel or crossing power systems can create induced AC voltage and current-density concerns. Evaluation has to consider personnel safety, coating condition, grounding or mitigation performance, operating configuration, and ongoing monitoring.

MONITORING

Remote monitoring

Remote monitoring can shorten the time between a change in system condition and organizational awareness. Its value is the decision time it creates, provided alarms, validation, ownership, and response procedures are defined.

FIELD STANDARD

The procedure, the work, and the record should tell the same story.

Qualified personnel, instrument condition, test method, site conditions, observations, exceptions, and follow-up should be traceable enough for another qualified person to understand what was done and why.

04 · INTEGRITY CONTEXT

Cathodic protection is one control inside a larger integrity system.

Coatings, CP, interference, inspection, direct assessment, excavations, repairs, operating history, records, and regulatory requirements describe different parts of the same integrity picture.

BARRIER + CURRENT

Coatings + CP

Coatings reduce the area exposed to the environment; cathodic protection addresses electrochemical activity where protection is needed. Coating deterioration changes current demand and can alter how the CP system performs over time.

DIRECT ASSESSMENT

ECDA

External Corrosion Direct Assessment connects pre-assessment, indirect inspection, direct examination, and post-assessment. CP and coating data are useful because they help prioritize where direct examination can most effectively validate the condition.

GROUND TRUTH

Integrity excavations & direct examination

Excavation findings test whether above-ground and indirect evidence accurately described the asset. Coating condition, corrosion morphology, measurements, photographs, repair decisions, and field notes become evidence for the next assessment.

REGULATORY CONTEXT

Requirements have to remain connected to field evidence.

Regulatory requirements establish obligations; procedures and engineering practices translate them into work. The record should show how the applicable requirement, field evidence, decision, and completed action connect.

05 · DATA CONFIDENCE & DIGITAL TOOLS

More corrosion data is useful only when the organization can trust and use it.

Remote monitoring, survey files, test-station readings, rectifier histories, coating surveys, excavation findings, GIS, inspection data, and integrity software can create a much stronger decision record when identifiers, timing, units, locations, procedures, and source quality are preserved.

DATA CONFIDENCE

Context is part of the measurement.

A value without location, time, instrument, method, units, operating condition, and asset identity loses decision value quickly. Data confidence begins before analytics.

SOFTWARE

Integrity software should reduce decision friction.

Useful systems help teams preserve history, compare evidence, identify changes, manage exceptions, support prioritization, and carry reasoning forward rather than simply collect another dataset.

AI-ASSISTED INTERPRETATION

AI can help find patterns; qualified judgment still owns the decision.

AI may help organize records, surface anomalies, compare histories, or identify relationships across large datasets. Confidence still depends on source quality, validation, uncertainty, operating context, and domain review.

OPERATIONAL TRUST

The evidence chain should survive the handoff.

Field technician, corrosion specialist, integrity engineer, operations, maintenance, contractor, manager, and auditor may all encounter the same decision at different points. The record should remain understandable as it moves between them.

SOURCE DISCIPLINE

Primary and authoritative sources carry the technical burden.

SOUBEL adds practical context and connects the pieces. Standards bodies, regulators, recognized research organizations, original technical literature, qualified engineering judgment, and company procedures remain the governing sources for asset-specific requirements and calculations.
