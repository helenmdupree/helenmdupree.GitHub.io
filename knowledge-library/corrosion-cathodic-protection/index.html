01

FOUNDATION

How do we tell if the metal is actually being protected?

Cathodic protection changes the electrochemical condition of the metal. Technicians take electrical readings, including the voltage difference between the structure and a reference electrode, and in some cases the current flowing through parts of the system.

What can change the meaning of those electrical readings?

Reference-electrode condition and placement, soil or electrolyte condition, current distribution, coating condition, interruption method, electrical continuity, interference, and operating state can all affect what a structure-to-electrolyte potential represents.

What makes the record defensible?

The record should preserve what was measured, how it was measured, the criterion or engineering basis used, the field conditions present, and the action that followed.

02

MATERIALS & EQUIPMENT

Each component has a job in the same protection system.

The useful question is what role each component plays, what can compromise that role, and what evidence tells the operator whether it is still doing its job.

Anodes & CP Systems

Anodes provide protective current through galvanic or impressed-current systems. Rectifiers & power systems provide and control DC power in impressed-current systems. Environment, current demand, geometry, output, connections, maintenance, and system condition all affect performance.

Testing & Field Measurement

Reference electrodes establish the comparison point for potential readings. Test stations & field connections provide access to structure leads, bonds, casings, coupons, and measurement points.

Cables, Connections & Remote Monitoring

Junction boxes organize circuits and monitoring points. RMUs provide remote visibility into selected conditions. Sensors extend condition visibility, while cables and connections preserve electrical continuity across the system.

03

FIELD EVIDENCE

How do we know what is happening in the field?

Surveys, electrical readings, monitoring systems, and field observations help show whether the corrosion-control system is performing as intended. Their value depends on how, where, and when the evidence was collected and whether the surrounding conditions are understood.

CIPS / CIS and routine monitoring

Close-interval surveys increase spatial resolution along the structure. Interpretation depends on reference-electrode control, stationing, interruption where appropriate, timing, repeatability, and comparison with other evidence.

AMPP reference · Close-interval potential surveys →

DCVG and coating surveys

Coating surveys help locate and characterize coating discontinuities and become more useful when results carry into prioritization, excavation planning, and comparison with observed condition.

AMPP reference · Aboveground coating-condition surveys →

DC and AC interference

Foreign CP systems, bonds, crossings, grounding systems, power corridors, and other current paths can change potentials and current distribution. Evaluation considers where current enters and leaves and how mitigation affects connected structures.

AMPP reference · Cathodic-protection interference →

Remote monitoring & digital visibility

Remote monitoring can shorten the time between a change in system condition and organizational awareness when alarms, validation, ownership, and response procedures are clear.

04

INTEGRITY CONTEXT

CP is one control inside a larger integrity system.

Coatings, CP, interference, direct assessment, excavation findings, repairs, inspection history, operating conditions, records, and regulatory requirements describe different parts of the same asset condition.

Coatings + CP

Coatings reduce the area exposed to the environment. Cathodic protection addresses electrochemical activity where protection is needed. Coating deterioration changes current demand and system performance over time.

ECDA

Pre-assessment, indirect inspection, direct examination, and post-assessment connect CP and coating evidence to prioritized field validation and the learning that follows.

Direct examination & excavation evidence

Coating condition, corrosion morphology, measurements, photographs, repair decisions, and field notes test what above-ground evidence predicted and strengthen the next assessment cycle.

05

DATA CONFIDENCE

Context is part of the measurement.

Survey files, test-station readings, rectifier histories, RMU data, coating surveys, excavation findings, GIS, inspection records, and integrity software become stronger evidence when identity, location, time, units, method, operating condition, and source remain intact.

Preserve the context

A number without location, time, instrument, method, units, operating condition, and asset identity loses decision value quickly. Data confidence starts before analytics and before AI.

Software & AI

Digital tools can preserve history, compare evidence, identify change, manage exceptions, and support prioritization. AI can surface patterns across large datasets, but qualified judgment still owns the decision and must understand source quality, validation, uncertainty, and operating context.
