PIPELINE INTEGRITY

Follow the evidence through the integrity cycle.

Pipeline integrity is the ability of a pipeline system to remain fit for its intended service and perform safely, reliably, and effectively under the operating and environmental conditions it is expected to encounter.

00 · FOUNDATION

Know the condition. Understand the threats.

Integrity is maintained over time by understanding what can threaten the pipeline, evaluating what is known about its condition, choosing assessment methods that fit the threat, acting on the evidence, and reassessing as conditions and information change.

Explore AIM Technical Knowledge

01 · ASSESSMENT SELECTION

Different threats require different evidence.

ILI, pressure testing, direct assessment, direct examination, NDE, and other methods answer different questions. The first discipline is matching the assessment method to the threat, the pipeline configuration, the information required, and the limitations of the method.

IN-LINE INSPECTION

Use the tool that can characterize the threat.

Tool technology, sizing capability, detection limits, inspection conditions, prior run history, validation results, and data quality determine what confidence can reasonably be placed in an ILI result.

PRESSURE TESTING

A pressure test answers a different integrity question.

Hydrostatic and other pressure-test approaches can demonstrate pressure resistance and expose certain defects, but they do not provide the same location-specific condition information as inspection or direct examination.

DIRECT ASSESSMENT

Use a threat-specific pathway when the method is appropriate.

ECDA, ICDA, and SCCDA are structured processes that integrate pre-assessment, indirect evidence, targeted examination, and post-assessment learning for specific threats.

02 · DIRECT ASSESSMENT

The letters aren’t the point. The mechanism is.

ECDA, ICDA, and SCCDA share a framework, but they are built around different damage mechanisms. The right method depends on the threat, the evidence available, and what the operator needs to confirm.

Same framework. Different physics.Use the wrong lens, and a technically complete assessment can still focus attention in the wrong place.
ECDA
External Corrosion

Where is external corrosion most likely, and what should be excavated?

Combines corrosion-control history, coating and environmental context, above-ground surveys, and excavation findings to prioritize external-corrosion indications.

DECISION FOCUSPrioritize indications, set excavation urgency, and compare predicted condition with what is actually found.
Technical detail

Under 49 CFR 192.925, ECDA is a four-step process: pre-assessment, indirect inspection, direct examination, and post-assessment.

CP context: cathodic-protection performance, coating condition, interference, electrical continuity, and field measurement quality can materially affect the evidence used in an ECDA program.

49 CFR 192.925 · ECDACorrosion & CP context
ICDA
Internal Corrosion

Where can electrolyte collect inside the pipe, and where should the operator look first?

Uses operating, elevation, flow, geometry, and other pipeline-specific data to identify locations where liquid or electrolyte is most likely to accumulate.

DECISION FOCUSDefine ICDA regions, predict liquid-holdup locations, examine the highest-risk areas, and validate the model against field findings.
Technical detail

For dry-gas ICDA under 49 CFR 192.927, the process includes pre-assessment, indirect inspection, detailed examination, and post-assessment monitoring.

Boundary: the federal dry-gas ICDA process is limited to normally dry natural-gas segments; additional requirements apply when electrolytes are normally present.

49 CFR 192.927 · ICDA
SCCDA
Stress Corrosion Cracking

Do operating history and environment support SCC susceptibility?

Brings together pipe, coating, CP, environment, operating stress, temperature, cyclic loading, and other susceptibility factors to target direct examination.

DECISION FOCUSIdentify susceptible segments, concentrate examination, characterize cracking, determine remediation, and set reassessment.
Technical detail

SCCDA uses indirect surveys and direct examination to test whether SCC is present and where the mechanism is most likely to be active.

Boundary: SCCDA is complementary to other SCC assessment methods such as ILI or hydrostatic testing with a spike test; it is not automatically a substitute for them.

49 CFR 192.929 · SCCDA
THE PRACTICAL DISTINCTION

ECDA follows evidence of external corrosion. ICDA follows conditions that can create internal corrosion. SCCDA follows susceptibility and evidence associated with stress-corrosion cracking.

03 · FIELD VALIDATION

From indication to observed condition.

Integrity excavations and direct examination are where an assessment prediction meets the physical asset. NDE, coating observations, measurements, photographs, samples, metallurgical or laboratory work, and repair records can confirm, refine, or contradict what the assessment suggested.

BEFORE THE DIG

Carry the assessment logic into the field.

The crew and technical team need to know what indication is being investigated, where it is expected, what measurements are required, and what evidence must be preserved.

AT THE PIPE

Document what is actually there.

Observed coating condition, corrosion morphology, crack characteristics, dimensions, NDE results, environmental observations, and photographs become part of the decision record.

AFTER THE DIG

Reconcile prediction with reality.

The value of direct examination is not only the repair. The finding should feed back into tool performance, prioritization logic, threat models, future assessment, and the asset record.

04 · INTEGRITY EVIDENCE & DECISION CONTINUITY

The next decision depends on what the organization remembers.

Assessment results, field findings, repairs, operating history, and prior decisions become more useful when they remain connected over time. Software can support that continuity, but it does not replace reliable source data, context, validation, or qualified judgment.

TRACEABILITY

Asset · Location · Date · Method · Conditions · Assumptions · Uncertainty · Disposition

DECISION SUPPORT

History · Inspection · Field evidence · Remediation · Risk · Follow-up

Software can preserve and connect the evidence. It cannot replace the quality of the data, the context, or the judgment applied to it.
