THE SECOND QUESTION

What should be done to preserve or restore required performance?

The goal is not more maintenance. It is the right action, at the right time, with enough preparation and feedback to improve the asset's ability to perform.

01

What condition are we actually managing?

Carry forward the evidence and context from Path 1. Clarify the observed condition, likely failure or degradation mechanism, uncertainty, criticality, and how the issue affects required performance.

02

What intervention is justified now?

Compare legitimate choices such as continued monitoring, inspection, operating adjustment, planned maintenance, repair, deferral, or replacement. Use consequence, timing, decision window, and reversibility to determine what action is proportionate.

03

Is the work ready to succeed?

Confirm people, qualifications, parts, tools, procedures, access, permits, operating windows, contractor support, responsibilities, and the reasoning that needs to travel with the work.

04

What did the work reveal?

Compare expected versus actual condition, capture field findings, verify whether performance improved, update assumptions, and carry useful learning into the next maintenance or reliability decision.

SOUBEL perspective: reliability insight creates value when it improves the quality or timing of action. Prediction by itself is not the outcome; the better maintenance decision is.

PRACTICAL RESOURCE

Reliability & Maintenance Decision Workbook

The workbook carries these four questions into a working decision record for technical, operational, planning, and field teams. It preserves the evidence, options, execution readiness, review triggers, field findings, and expected-versus-actual results behind the decision so the reasoning does not disappear with the people who made it. Years later, another qualified person should still be able to understand what was known, why the action was chosen, what uncertainty remained, and what happened afterward.

Decision Continuity: preserve enough of the reasoning behind an operational decision that the next person can understand it, challenge it, and learn from what happened.

Established reliability and asset-management practice, organized for practical use.

THE TEST

Did the intervention improve the asset's ability to perform?

If the result differs from expectation, the difference is new evidence. Carry it forward before comparing repair, replacement, continued operation, or other lifecycle choices.
