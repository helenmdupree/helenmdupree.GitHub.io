THE THIRD QUESTION

Which option creates the strongest operating value over the period being considered?

The comparison is stronger when every credible option is evaluated on the same economic and operating basis rather than judging one choice by its invoice and another by its risk or remaining life.

01

What options are realistically available?

Define the legitimate choices before comparing them. Depending on the condition and operating need, those may include continued monitoring, additional evidence, operating changes, planned intervention, repair, mitigation, deferral, or replacement.

02

What does each option require now?

Compare immediate expenditure, labor, materials, specialist or contractor support, outage requirements, downtime, production or throughput effects, operating constraints, and the effect on other planned work.

03

What does each option preserve or expose over time?

Consider remaining useful life, reliability, capacity, maintenance burden, future flexibility, cost of deferral, and credible safety, environmental, regulatory, production, financial, customer, or reputation consequences.

04

What uncertainty could change the comparison?

Record the assumptions that carry the most weight, what is still unknown, what evidence could change the economics, how reversible each option is, and when the comparison should be revisited.

SOUBEL perspective: lifecycle economics is not simply about choosing the lowest-cost option. It is about making the trade-offs visible enough to explain what the organization expects to spend, preserve, expose, defer, and keep possible.

REFERENCE BASIS

Established asset-management and life-cycle-value principles, organized for practical use.

This page and worksheet draw on established asset-management, life-cycle-cost, integrity-management, reliability, and risk principles. They support leadership comparison and documentation and are intended to complement engineering analysis, formal life-cycle costing, company procedures, qualified technical judgment, and applicable regulation.

Selected basis: ISO 55001:2024; ISO 15686-5:2017; Institute of Asset Management · Life Cycle Value Realisation; and PHMSA integrity-management guidance for pipeline-specific risk and consequence context.

THE TEST

Can the organization explain why this option creates stronger value than the alternatives?

A defensible lifecycle comparison keeps the evidence, assumptions, cost, operating impact, consequence exposure, remaining life, and review conditions connected well enough for someone else to understand the choice later.
